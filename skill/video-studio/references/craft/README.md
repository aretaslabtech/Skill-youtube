**The 10 rules that matter most**
1. Frame 0 is the hook and the cover: the VO starts by 0.2 s, the claim is on screen by 1.5 s, and no logo appears in the first 2 s.
2. Narration, on-screen text and visual must say one thing. On-screen text is at most 6 words and never the spoken sentence.
3. Write three hooks of different types, score them out of 12, and rewrite if the best scores under 8.
4. Something changes state every 1.5–3 s on a Short. A drifting background is not a change.
5. The payoff lands at 65–85 % of the runtime, and the last frame rhymes with the first so the Short loops.
6. Teach concrete before abstract: name the term only after the example has shown the mechanism.
7. Only what's being explained moves. Use one `voltage` accent per frame, no full-frame gradients on dark, and 2–4 % grain if a gradient is unavoidable.
8. On 1080×1920 the visual fills the frame: meaning in y 200–1440 (x ≤ 850 below y 1150), captions in y 1440–1600, and the bottom continues the scene (`platform-specs.md` §1, B18).
9. Use real assets and real numbers only, with no invented proof. Code on screen must have run, and proof comes from your own records or the store consoles.
10. Use one specific CTA after the payoff. On a Short, set the related-video link. Use at most 5 hashtags on Instagram and 3–5 on YouTube.
